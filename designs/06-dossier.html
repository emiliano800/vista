The whiteboard version versus the recorded version.

Most process maps in a diligence deck come from a two-hour interview with the controller. They are honest, and they are wrong in the places that matter.

From interviews

  • “We enter invoices in QuickBooks.” No count of how many come from PDFs by hand.
  • “Matching is quick.” Rework loops are invisible in memory.
  • “Approvals take a day.” An average nobody measured.
  • Savings estimated from headcount and a benchmark.

From recorded work

  • 42 cross-app pastes, Acrobat → QuickBooks, 3.1 observed hours a week.
  • Match PO reworked 6 times in 40 cases, each loop timestamped.
  • Mean wait Enter invoice → Match PO: 1 h 12 m, per edge, per variant.
  • Every figure resolves to steps with a stated source: observed, rule, filled, human.

A short, auditable method.

Two weeks at a handful of desks. Redaction on device. A stdlib-only engine you can read in an afternoon.

  1. week 0

    Consent and scope

    Employees see the recorder, the overlay, and the mute list. Private apps and keywords are excluded before recording starts.

  2. weeks 1–2

    Record and annotate

    Clicks, focus, copy-paste links and window titles are logged and redacted locally. Employees note phone, paper and meetings.

  3. on stop

    Discover

    Steps, cases, a directly-follows graph with waits and rework, exported as CSV and XES for any process-mining tool.

  4. week 3

    Memo and interview

    Ranked candidates with observed hours, plus the open questions for the parts screens cannot see.
